# Answers

1. Two terms: "static" and "current" (transcript [00:12]-[00:36]; board am00_81s.png shows Electricity -> Static / Current). Static = study of electric charge at rest; current = electric charge in motion ("static stands for stationary, current stands for motion", [02:13]-[02:18]). Electricity itself = electric charge ([00:48]-[00:58]).

2. Symbol capital Q (board "Electric charge (Q)", am00_81s.png; [01:10]). SI unit coulomb, written "[C]" on the board (am01_244s.png: "S.I. coulomb [C]"). Spoken "abbreviated as capital ..." is a gap in the transcript ([01:30]); the letter C comes from the frame. Named after physicist Charles Augustine Coulomb ([01:34]; transcript spells it "column").

3. "Current electricity: Energy associated with a moving charge" (board, am01_244s.png; spoken [02:56]-[03:03], repeated [03:18], [13:42], [15:11]).

4. H.L = higher level (spoken [07:30]; board labels "H.L" on cylinder A and "L.L" on B, am05_896s.png). L.L = lower level ([07:32]-[07:38]). S = the stop (stopcock) in the side tube connecting the cylinders ([07:02]). Dotted line across both cylinders = the common level the water reaches, i.e. both levels equipotential; no further flow ([08:28]-[08:52]). Saw: dotted lines in both cylinders (am05, am04); the "equipotential" interpretation is from the transcript.

5. A is the wider cylinder and is on the left (am05_896s.png: A label under the wide left vessel, B under the narrow right one; transcript [06:47]-[07:08] "broader ... narrow", "cylinder a ... cylinder b" with A as the high-level one).

6. Starting temperatures 80 deg C (hot) and 20 deg C (cold) ([09:24], [09:30]; board "80C", "20C", am04_733s.png). Surroundings: isolated / insulated, no heat in or out ([09:35]-[09:50]). Final state: thermal equilibrium ([10:41]). Final common temperature: spoken "10 degrees celsius" ([10:35]), which is not between 20 and 80 so is likely a misstatement or mis-transcription; on the board it is written as "T deg C" (circled T°C, am04_733s.png, am05_896s.png), a symbol not a number.

7. Early physicists took the proton (positive charge) as the moving one, because the electron had not yet been discovered ([05:10]-[05:40], [14:16]-[14:30]; "ampere and walter ... dissecting a frog" is the transcript's garbled account). Symbols: spoken "proton: one plus one; electron: e, 0, minus one" ([04:26]-[04:32]), i.e. superscript 1 / subscript +1 for the proton and e with superscript 0 / subscript -1 for the electron. Board: only partly visible at the far right edge, cut off (am03/am04 show "p..." and "+1" fragments), so the exact board writing, including the proton's letter, is insufficient evidence from the frames. The above is inferred from speech.

8. 1.5 volt ([12:32]; board "1.5V" circled, am05_896s.png). Positive end = high potential ([12:45]-[12:55]). Drawing (am05): a cell rectangle with "+" at the left end labelled "H.L" with a tick, and "-" at the right end labelled "L.L" with a tick; a small dot/nub on the left end.
