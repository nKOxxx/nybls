## Q2 — Three elements, meaning, order
**Label: S**

**Answer:** Logos, ethos, pathos. Logos = the intellectual side / content of your talk, "the scientific meat". Pathos = reaching them emotionally, connecting on a human level, why the topic matters. Ethos = convincing them you are the right person / qualified to deliver the message. Order in the talk: pathos first, then ethos, and only then logos.

**Evidence:**
- [01:01] "You need the logos, the ethos, and the pathos."
- [01:04] logos = "the intellectual side, the content of your talk"
- [01:36]–[01:43] "before you get to that logos part... We need to open them up emotionally with pathos. And then with ethos, we need to convince them that you're the right person"
- [01:54]–[02:19] pathos "reach them emotionally"; ethos "Why you're the right person, why you're qualified"; "only when you have hit both the pathos and the ethos are you in the right place to communicate the logos, which is really the scientific meat"
- Supporting frames: g_00100s.png (triad + "OPEN THEM UP EMOTIONALLY"), g_00110s.png ("REACH THEM EMOTIONALLY"), g_00130s.png ("WHY ARE YOU THE RIGHT PERSON?").

## Q7 — How to close the talk
**Label: S**

**Answer:** Go full circle: when you bring them back to the front door, return to the anecdote/story you opened with (the human face of the data, or the story about your profile or the research), so the talk engages emotionally at the end as it did at the beginning. He also says to pepper anecdotes through the talk, especially toward the end of results and future research.
