**2. Jubayr ibn Mut'im hadith [07:37-08:50].**
Saw (transcript): the man says "we seek intercession with you to Allah, and we seek intercession with Allah against you" [08:00-08:04]. The Prophet turns, says "Woe to you, do you know who Allah is?" twice [08:18-08:20], then glorifies Allah (subhan Allah repeatedly) until his face visibly changes [08:22-08:31]. He then says: "Allah is above His Throne, His Throne is above His heavens, and His heavens are like this", with his fingers and palm spread in a dome shape [08:36-08:50]. So he describes Allah as above His Throne, which is above the heavens.

**3. Rain-cloud hadith distances [09:05-10:09].**
Cites narrations from Abbas ibn Abd al-Muttalib, Abu Hurayra, and (mawquf) Ibn Mas'ud. The cloud: "sahab, muzn, anan". Distances stated, each 500 years' journey:
- between the earth and the lowest heaven [09:30-09:35, 09:50];
- between each heaven and the next [09:54];
- the thickness of each heaven [10:00];
- between the bottom and the top of the Throne [10:03].
(The speaker contrasts this with the "hundreds of metres" people imagine [09:42].)

**4. Benefits of knowing Allah's greatness [15:14 onward]:**
1. He worships only Allah alone: no fear, hope, love, humility, or bowing to anyone else; any attachment to others is only as far as the means Allah gave [15:14-15:49].
2. He is not vain about his worship, because Allah is greater. The Ahmad hadith: a man prostrate on his face from birth to death would still find his worship small on the Day of Resurrection [15:52-16:37].
3. He is freed from servitude to others and to desire; true freedom is servitude to Allah [17:49-18:42].
4. He regards whatever he gives up for Allah as small and spends for Him gladly, by analogy with the thorn/barefoot walk and the job salary [18:44-20:08]. Prophets are the steadiest because they know Allah best [20:22-20:53], and the Prophet chose death and meeting Allah [20:58-21:14].
5. He gains certainty, strength, faith, and reliance (tawakkul) [26:30, 30:23-30:27].
6. He says "inna lillahi" at loss and sees all as Allah's [24:33-25:00].
7. He worships Allah in private as in public, e.g. night prayer behind a closed door [30:54-31:28].
8. He avoids sins even when alone, because Allah is the Watcher [31:30-32:17].
9. He does not relapse from the truth under trial, and is not swayed by crowds; Ibrahim was "an ummah" [32:34-34:15].

**5. Why Allah does not take revenge on those who curse Him [26:56-30:23].**
Reasons:
- People wrongly assume Allah is like themselves and must defend Himself [27:29, 30:32-30:51]. The speaker notes this drives some to atheism [27:22].
- Allah is not reached by anyone's harm and is not hurt by anyone. He cites the hadith qudsi "you will never reach My harm so as to harm Me" [29:18-29:36], and says "whoever does good, it is for himself" [29:22].
- Non-retaliation is a delay (imhal), not weakness or approval [29:55-30:01].
Analogies:
- A 3-year-old child insults you: you would not hit him, maybe give him sweets, since you are too great to engage him. A 20-30-year-old insulting you is different, because rivalry begins [27:47-28:25].
- A mosquito bites you, or an ant stings you: you do not chase it [28:33-29:11].
- You and your whole world are worth less to Allah than a mosquito's wing [29:42-29:50].
(Counter-analogies in 23:03-23:19: insulting your father or a ruler and saying "I didn't mean it" is not accepted.)

**6. Can a person worship nothing? [17:42-18:35].**
No. Man was created a worshipper, not a worshipped one, and must be a servant [17:35-17:46]. The options: "choose your god": Allah, something other than Allah, or your own desire (hawa) [17:49-17:54]. Those who think they are free because they worship no one are in fact worshipping themselves/desire [17:57-18:08]. Verse cited: "Have you seen the one who takes his desire as his god?" (Al-Jathiyah 45:23; the transcript gives only the verse text at [18:11], not the reference, so the sura/number is my inference).

**7. Lecture title and root cause of shirk.**
Title: insufficient evidence. No title is spoken in the transcript and the single frame shows no legible title text. (The theme is the greatness of Allah / knowing Allah's measure, inferred from the content.)
Root cause: ignorance of Allah's greatness, so one gives greatness to others. "No shirk or kufr occurs among people except through ignorance of Allah's greatness, and this greatness is given to other than Allah" [06:15-06:23]. On idol worship specifically [06:58-07:09]: "the reason is that you were ignorant of Allah, so you magnified other than Allah", citing "they did not esteem Allah as He should be esteemed" (Al-Hajj 22:74, verse text only at [07:09]) after the fly parable [06:44-06:57].

**8. Ranking of cursing Allah [21:30-22:53].**
He ranks it worse than idol worship and worse than atheism.
- Worse than idolatry: idolaters magnified idols as a branch of magnifying Allah, while the curser has belittled Allah entirely [21:58-22:16]. In a society where cursing Allah is common, idol worship would be lighter in Allah's sight [22:20-22:23].
- Worse than atheism: the atheist denies knowledge of any creator, while the curser affirms the Creator and then curses Him, which is stubbornness, defiance, and challenge [22:29-22:42]. He calls it the worst, or among the worst, kinds of kufr [22:46-22:53].
- He adds that the sinner at the moment of sin keeps a spark of faith, but the one who curses Allah has no faith left [23:45-24:19].
