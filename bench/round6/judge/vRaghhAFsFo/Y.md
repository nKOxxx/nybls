## 3. Benefits of a good first impression
He lists three [07:36]–[08:57]:
1. **People see you more favourably / ethos reinforces logos** [07:49]–[08:16]: if they find you elegant at first sight they read every word, gesture and posture as elegance; if they find you funny they laugh even at your stupidest jokes, because it is you, not the joke, that is funny.
2. **People remember you** [08:20]–[08:39]: who you are, what you said and how you said it; they solicit you, invite you to their parties and after-works "parce que vous êtes cool".
3. **People forgive your mistakes more easily** [08:42]–[09:40], and even invent excuses for your bad behaviour. Illustration: if he blanked and forgot his speech right now, the audience with a good impression would say "ce n'est pas grave, ça arrive"; with a bad impression they would see it as disrespect — "ce que j'ai fait n'a pas changé, c'est votre interprétation qui change".

## 4. Ethos, reputation, and the reputation example
- **Ethos** [06:38]–[06:51]: a notion from oratory referring to **the credibility of the speaker** — "même si vous avez de belles phrases et que ce que vous dites est vrai, personne ne va écouter si vous n'êtes pas crédible."
- **Reputation** [11:10]–[11:17]: **"ce que les gens disent de vous lorsque vous n'êtes pas dans la salle"** (what people say about you when you are not in the room).
- **Example** [11:19]–[11:36]: **Elon Musk** — if he walked into this room in pyjamas and stammered through every speech, you would still listen, "parce que c'est Elon Musk… sa réputation le précède."
(He adds [10:08]–[11:06] that reputation is built on a "preuve tangible de votre image de marque", e.g. a diploma from the top university in the field — a piece of paper proves nothing about your intellect, but it is better to have one than not.)
Evidence: transcript [06:38]–[11:36].

## 5. No reputation or diploma yet: what to do, and the body-language elements
Framing [12:12]–[12:39]: fresh graduate, no solid reputation, no tangible proof. First lever: **your look** — clothes and accessories, the first thing people see [12:41]–[12:50] (see Q6). Then **a body language that inspires confidence before you speak** [15:30]–[18:30], in this order:
1. **Posture** [15:45]–[16:09]: back straight, shoulders square, chin parallel to the ground ("dos bien droit, épaules carrées, menton parallèle au sol"); stop hunching over your phone, "redressez-vous et reprenez votre couronne".
2. **Walk** [16:12]–[16:31]: a more poised, calmer, more charismatic walk — slow your steps a little.
3. **Gestures** [16:40]–[17:51]: gestures that inspire authority and confidence — take up space. Mammals that feel weak curl up and take as little space as possible; a powerful animal takes space (fighting cocks ruffle their feathers). "Arrêtez de vous emprisonner dans cette case… ouvrez-vous au monde, prenez de l'espace."
4. **Facial expression** [17:54]–[18:30]: a genuine, open smile ("un très beau et très franc sourire"); don't wait for the dentist, smile now. He has the audience smile at their neighbour on three.
Recap at [18:33]–[18:55]: smile, open gestures, poised walk, square shoulders, straight back, chin parallel to the floor, the right dress code, a good reputation, tangible proof of your brand.

## 6. Dress, and when to make the effort
- Not "always dress formally" [12:55]–[13:02]: "Pas du tout."
- **Be comfortable in your clothes first** [13:05]–[13:38] — otherwise it fails; the hole-in-the-sock anecdote (fold the sock under the foot, still feel everyone can see it: impostor syndrome).
- **Clothing is a code** [13:40]–[14:36]: no shorts and flip-flops at a wedding, no three-piece suit at the beach. His own lesson: jeans/T-shirt/sneakers in an office where everyone wears suit and tie, nobody takes him seriously unless they already know him; a three-piece suit with a field team in jeans, they won't like him — "parce que je n'ai pas les codes". Ask: do you have the codes of your environment?
- **When to adapt** [14:40]–[15:18]: not "always" — you adapt to your **environment, not to other people**, and you do it **only when it matters to you**, when you know an extra effort can help you reach your goal. Example: maybe no effort for the family reunion where everyone already hates you; yes for the job interview, because that is important to you.
Evidence: transcript [12:41]–[15:18]. (He himself is in a white shirt and brown waistcoat/trousers, sheet_000 [00:35]–[00:55].)
