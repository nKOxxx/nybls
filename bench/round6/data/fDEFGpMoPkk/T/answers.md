1. Two terms: "static" and "current" (00:12-00:36). Per him: static = studies of electric charge at rest ("static stands for stationary", 01:55-02:01, 02:13); current = electric charge in motion ("current stands for motion", 02:04-02:18). He also says the term "electricity" itself simply means electric charge (00:48-00:58).

2. Symbol: capital Q (01:10). SI unit: coulomb (transcript spells it "column", 01:24-01:31). Abbreviation: he says "abbreviated as capital ..." (01:30-01:34) but the letter is not in the transcript (audio-only gap). Inferred from general knowledge: C. Named after physicist Charles Augustin Coulomb (01:33-01:34, "charles augustine column").

3. "Current electricity is the energy associated with a moving charge" (02:56-03:18; repeated 13:42-13:44, 14:05-14:08, 15:07-15:13, where he calls it the kinetic energy associated with the moving charge).

4. HL = higher level (07:30, "hl stands for higher level"). S = stop (07:02, "s stands for the stop", a stop/valve in the connecting side tube). LL: the transcript never says "LL" explicitly; he says cylinder B has water "at the lower level" (07:35-07:38), so LL = lower level is an inference. The dotted (red) line marks the final common water level, i.e. the two levels have become equipotential, at which point water stops flowing (08:03-08:52). The transcript does not say explicitly that the line is drawn across both cylinders, but that is consistent.

5. Insufficient evidence. He says one cylinder is broader and one narrow (06:47-06:55) and names them A and B (07:04-07:08) but the transcript does not say which is which or which side is left/right.

Final common temperature, spoken: "10 degrees celsius" (10:32-10:35) - this is physically impossible (must lie between 20 and 80), so it is probably a slip or transcription error. What he wrote on the board: insufficient evidence (audio-only; not mentioned).
