## 1. Introduction and opener
**Answer.** Write your own introduction and deliver it to the host in advance, because the host will be "bragging about you" and building your credibility for you [00:41–01:02, 01:28–01:31]. Since the credibility work is done before you walk on, don't waste the opening: jump straight into something impactful — a story, a quote, or a statistic with punch [01:16–01:23, 01:33–01:37].
**Evidence.** Transcript [00:37]–[01:37].

## 2. Too loud through the mic
**Answer.** Don't worry about being too loud; "too loud is better than too soft" [01:39–01:44]. You'll hear your own voice echo and be tempted to go quieter — he urges you not to [01:49–02:01]. If you really are too loud, the audiovisual guy will turn your microphone down [02:03–02:06]. The bigger risk is being too quiet and having people miss your message [02:08–02:20]. Also speak up more than you think in mid-sized (30–50 person) rooms where you may not be mic'd [02:27–03:00].
**Evidence.** Transcript [01:39]–[03:00].

## 3. Walking routine with a wireless mic
**Answer.** Routine: (1) take two or three steps, then stop [03:56]; (2) tell a story / make a point [03:59]; (3) ask a question [04:01]; (4) take two or three steps back or move to another place on stage [04:04–04:05]; repeat "a few times" over a 20–40 minute talk — don't march the whole time [03:49–04:16]. The "Walk the Walk" slide condenses it to: Take a few steps; Stop to make a point or tell a story; Move on, engage your audience [03:33–03:49]. Mic caution: be careful not to bump your mic and not to trip over the cord [03:33–03:37]. Also: don't touch or lean on the podium [03:04–03:24].
**Evidence.** Transcript [03:04]–[04:20]; sheet_001.png (tiles 03:36, 03:49) and sheet_004.png (tile 03:33) showing the "Walk the Walk" slide.

## 4. 30-minute slot
**Answer.** End at 27 minutes — "if you're given a 30 minute window end with 27 minutes, don't go over" [05:47–05:52]. Reasons: it's better to leave space at the end for Q&A and networking than to use the whole allotment [05:58–06:04], and you don't want people getting up and leaving early — often there's a session right after you, or worse, it's lunch hour and people walk out as you're closing [06:08–06:18]. (For a 60–90 min slot, end closer to an hour [05:53–05:56].)
**Evidence.** Transcript [05:33]–[06:25].

## 6. Looking down
**Answer.** Mistake: speakers fall into the trap of looking down instead of facing the audience [07:34–07:40]. Cause: the podium often has a display showing your slide, and there are confidence monitors on the stage floor below you showing your slides — invisible to the audience, so first-timers may not know they're there [07:40–08:01]. Acceptable amount: "an occasional glance down is all right" to check which slide you're on, but don't glance down too much — face the audience and keep eye contact [08:02–08:23]. (He also gives the obvious one: don't look back at the screen [07:22–07:29].)
**Evidence.** Transcript [07:11]–[08:23].
