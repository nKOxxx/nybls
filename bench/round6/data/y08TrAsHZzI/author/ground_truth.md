## Q1 - label S
Answer: Write your introduction and deliver it to the meeting host, because the host brags about you and builds your credibility. Since credibility is already built, jump right into something impactful: a story, a quote, or a statistic with punch.
Evidence: transcript [00:41]-[01:37] ("make sure your introduction is written and delivered to the host", "jump right into a story or ... a quote or a statistic").
Partial: only "send the host an intro" or only "open with story/quote/stat".

## Q2 - label S
Answer: Do not worry about being too loud; speak up, too loud is better than too soft. If you really are too loud, the audiovisual person will turn your microphone down. (Also: speak louder than you think in mid-sized rooms of 30-50 people, even un-mic'd.)
Evidence: [01:39]-[03:04] ("the audiovisual guy will turn down your microphone", "Too loud, always better than not loud enough").
Partial: says "speak louder" without the AV-turns-it-down point.

## Q3 - label S
Answer: Walk away from the podium; take two or three steps, stop, tell a story or make a point, ask a question, then take two or three steps back or to another spot; repeat a few times over a 20-40 minute talk. Don't march constantly. Be careful not to bump the mic or trip over the cord (ideally wireless).
Evidence: [03:26]-[04:20] ("Take two or three steps and then stop. Tell a story, make a point. And then ask a question. And then take two or three steps back"; "don't bump your mic and you don't trip over the cord"). Slide (grid g_00220s.png "Walk the Walk") restates: take a few steps / stop to make a point or tell a story / move on, engage your audience.
Partial: steps/stop/point without the question or the mic/cord caution.

## Q4 - label S
Answer: End at about 27 minutes (a bit early). Reason: leave space at the end for Q&A and networking, and avoid people leaving early because another session or lunch is right behind you. (For 1-1.5 hr slots, end closer to an hour.)
Evidence: [05:33]-[06:25] ("If you're given a 30 minute window end with 27 minutes"; "better to leave a big space at the end for ... questions and answers and some networking").
Partial: 27 minutes only.

## Q6 - label S
Answer: Looking down at displays: podium screens and floor monitors on stage that show your slides/content, invisible to the audience. An occasional glance is fine, but don't look down too much; face the audience and make eye contact. (Also don't look back at the screen.)
Evidence: [07:11]-[08:23] ("an occasional glance down is all right, but just remember, face your audience"). Slide g_00500s.png "Face the Audience" repeats it.
Partial: names looking down without cause, or the occasional-glance limit without the displays.
