It opens mid-lecture ([00:00] "تفضل مشكلة", then the standard greeting). The Arabic Salafi-style content and the closing address to "sisters" [34:42] fit a Sunni Arabic preacher, but I cannot name him, the city/country or the Hijri date from this transcript.

2. Hadith of Jubayr ibn Mut'im (Abu Dawud) [07:37-08:50]. Transcript: the man says "we seek intercession with you to Allah and with Allah to you". The Prophet answers "Woe to you! Do you know who Allah is?" (repeated). He glorifies God repeatedly ("Subhan Allah"), until the change shows in his face. Then he says: "Allah is above His Throne, and His Throne is above His heavens, and His heavens are like this", and he spread his fingers with his palm in the shape of a dome (qubba) [08:38-08:50]. The speaker's gloss: the man was ignorant of Allah's greatness.

3. Rain-cloud hadith (attributed by the speaker to Abbas ibn Abd al-Muttalib, Abu Hurayra, and as mawquf to Ibn Mas'ud) [09:05-10:09]. The Prophet sees a cloud and asks what it is ("sahab", "muzn", "'anan"). He states:
- between the earth and the nearest heaven: a journey of 500 years;
- between each heaven and the next: 500 years;
- the thickness of each heaven: 500 years;
- between the bottom and top of the Throne: 500 years;
and Allah is above the Throne.

4. Benefits of knowing Allah's greatness [15:14-15:49 onward, to 34:15]:
 (1) Tawhid: worship, fear, hope, love and humility are directed only to Allah [15:14-15:49].
 (2) No pride in one's worship, since Allah is greater. He cites Ahmad: even a man who fell on his face from birth till death would belittle his worship on the Day of Judgment [15:52-16:31].
 (3) Freedom from servitude to others and to one's desire; true freedom is servitude to Allah [17:49-18:42].
 (4) Sacrifice for Allah's sake seems small; one gives in proportion to the one revered. Analogy: a job with a high salary; the Prophets are the firmest because they know Allah best [18:44-21:25].
 (5) Certainty (yaqin) [30:23-30:28].
 (6) Worships properly, in secret as in public (night prayer behind a closed door) [30:54-31:28].
 (7) Avoids sins even when alone, because Allah is the Watcher [31:30-32:17].
 (8) Does not relapse or turn back under affliction; stays on the truth even if alone, like Ibrahim "was a nation" [32:39-34:15].
 (9) Also stated: the strength, faith and reliance on Allah that come from reflecting on the cosmos [26:30]. Handling loss with "inna lillahi wa inna ilayhi raji'un" [24:33-24:55] is presented as a result of knowing Him.

5. Why Allah does not take revenge on cursers [26:48-30:51]. Transcript: people ask why Allah does not defend Himself, and some are driven to atheism [27:05-27:25]. The speaker's reasons:
- The questioner assumes Allah is like a human who defends himself; revenge depends on the greatness of the one insulting you (nidda, "rivalry") [27:29-28:25].
- Analogy 1: a 3-year-old child insults you; you give him sweets instead of hitting him. If an adult does it, it is different [27:47-28:25].
- Analogy 2: a mosquito bites you, or an ant stings you; you do not chase it [28:33-29:11].
- Allah is not harmed or hurt by anyone ("O My servants, you will never reach My harm so as to harm Me", hadith qudsi) [29:15-29:36].
- You and your whole world are not worth a mosquito's wing to Allah [29:42-29:50].
- The delay is respite (imhal), not weakness [29:55-30:01].
- Allah is "nothing like Him" [30:51].

6. No: a person cannot choose to worship nothing [17:35-18:33]. Man was created to be a worshipper, not worshipped, and must be a servant. The options are Allah, someone other than Allah, or one's desire (hawa) [17:49-17:54]. One who thinks that not worshipping anyone makes him free is in fact worshipping his self/desire. The verse cited is "Have you seen him who has taken his desire as his god?" [18:09-18:11] (Quran 45:23 per my own knowledge; the transcript does not give the reference).

7. Title: insufficient evidence; the transcript gives no title. The theme is "knowing/magnifying Allah's greatness" (ta'zim Allah). Root cause of shirk and idolatry [06:15-07:09]: ignorance of Allah's greatness; "the reason is that you were ignorant of Allah, so you magnified other than Allah", and no shirk or kufr occurs except through that ignorance, which places greatness in others.

8. Ranking [21:30-22:56]: Shirk is the worst evil, and cursing/insulting Allah is worse than it. Cursing is worse than idol-worship: idolaters magnified idols as a branch of magnifying Allah, while the curser has belittled Allah entirely, so his kufr is more severe [21:56-22:20]. Societies with idol-worship are better in Allah's sight than those where He is cursed [22:20-22:29]. It is even worse than atheism [22:29-22:36]: the atheist denies a Creator out of ignorance of Him, while the curser affirms the Creator and insults Him, which is obstinacy, defiance and challenge [22:36-22:42]. He calls it among the severest forms of kufr [22:46-22:50] and says no faith remains in one who curses Allah [24:15]. Note that the "ignorance" of the atheist is the speaker's characterisation.
