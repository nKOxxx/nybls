# Answers (video qVIzSMux720; Arabic lecture, transcript carries nearly everything; 1 sheet opened for the on-screen caption)

1. Speaker: Sheikh Abdulaziz al-Tarefe (الشيخ عبد العزيز الطريفي). Held in Irbid, Jordan, in Sha'ban 1434 AH. Evidence: on-screen caption in sheet_000.png tiles 00:03-00:23: 'محاضرة "تعظيم الله عز وجل" للشيخ عبد العزيز الطريفي / عقدت في الأردن، مدينة إربد، في شعبان ١٤٣٤'. (Gregorian ~June-July 2013 is my conversion, not stated.)

2. [07:35-08:50] The Prophet turns to him and says "Woe to you, do you know who Allah is?" (repeated), then glorifies Allah ("Subhan Allah" repeatedly) until it shows on his face. He then says Allah is above His Throne, His Throne is above His heavens, and His heavens are like this, spreading his fingers/palm in the shape of a dome. The speaker's gloss: the man sought intercession out of ignorance of Allah's greatness. (Transcript: "Allah is above his throne, above his heavens".)

3. [09:16-10:09] Rain cloud hadith (Abbas, Abu Hurayra, Ibn Mas'ud mawquf): between the earth and the nearest heaven is a 500-year journey; between each heaven and the next is 500 years; the thickness of each heaven is 500 years; between the lowest and highest part of the Throne is 500 years; Allah is above the Throne.

4. Benefits (speaker's list, [15:14]-[34:15]): (1) tawhid: worship, fear, hope, love only for Allah, attachment to others only as a means [15:14]; (2) not being deceived by one's own worship, since it is small next to Allah's greatness (Ahmad hadith: someone prostrate on his face from birth to death would belittle his worship on the Day of Judgement) [15:52]; (3) true freedom: freed from servitude to others and to desire [~17:49-18:44]; (4) belittling what one spends/sacrifices for Allah, with the job-salary and thorn analogies, hence willingness to sacrifice and endure trials; prophets steadfast (Prophet chose death and meeting Allah over worldly kingship) [18:44-21:14]; (5) worshipping Allah in private as in public, e.g. night prayer behind a closed door [30:54-31:28]; (6) avoiding sins when alone [31:30-32:20]; (7) not relapsing from the truth under affliction, and staying with Allah even if alone (Ibrahim "was a nation") [32:34-34:15]; also certainty/yaqin [30:23], and saying "inna lillahi" at loss [24:33]; (8) humility/belittling oneself and understanding Allah's wisdom in not punishing [26:38].

5. [26:56-30:51] Reasons: people wrongly assume Allah is like humans who retaliate to defend themselves; one avenges only in proportion to the greatness of the opponent. Analogies: a 3-year-old child insulting you (you might give him sweets), vs an adult peer (rivalry begins); a mosquito/gnat bites or an ant stings and you do not chase it; the ant might think it harmed you and you did not respond. Allah is not reached by anyone's harm or hurt (hadith qudsi "you will never reach My harm so as to harm Me" [29:34]); you and your whole world are worth less than a mosquito's wing to Allah [29:46]; non-punishment is a respite (imhal) not a lack of anger; Allah is "nothing like Him" and not like man [30:51].

6. [17:35-18:44] No: man was created to be a worshipper; he must be servile to something. Three options: "choose your god": Allah, other than Allah, or your own desire (hawa). One who thinks he is free by worshipping none is actually worshipping himself/desire. Verse: "Have you seen him who takes his desire as his god?" (أفرأيت من اتخذ إلهه هواه, Al-Jathiya 45:23; surah number is my identification, the speaker only quotes the verse).

7. Title: "Ta'dhim Allah 'azza wa jall" (تعظيم الله عز وجل, Glorifying/Magnifying Allah), per the on-screen caption (sheet tiles 00:03-00:23) and video title. Root cause of shirk/idolatry [07:00-07:09]: ignorance of Allah, so one magnifies something other than Allah ("you ignored Allah so you magnified other than Allah"; they did not esteem Allah as is His due); also [06:15] every shirk and kufr is due to ignorance of Allah's greatness and giving that greatness to others.

8. [21:35-22:53] He ranks cursing/insulting Allah as worse than idolatry ("greater than paganism"), and worse than atheism. Why: idolaters venerated Allah and made idols as a branch of that veneration, so they at least magnify Him; the one who curses has belittled Allah entirely. A society with idol worship would be less odious to Allah than one where He is cursed. The atheist denies knowledge of any creator, whereas the one who curses affirms the Creator and insults Him, which is stubbornness, defiance and challenge. He concludes it is among the most severe, if not the most severe, types of kufr. The "I didn't mean it" excuse fails, as with insulting a father or ruler [23:03]; one who curses Allah has no faith left [24:15].
