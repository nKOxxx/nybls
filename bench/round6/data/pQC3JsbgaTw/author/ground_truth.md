## Q2 — Meaning of 0; nut and first fret
**Label: S**

**Answer:** A 0 means an open string — pluck the string without pressing down any fret. The 1 is produced by pressing the string between the nut (the white piece of plastic) and the first fret (the piece of metal).

**Evidence:**
- Transcript [00:38]–[00:46]: "a zero appears on each string as I pluck it. That zero represents an open string. It means that I'm not pressing down any frets."
- Transcript [01:16]–[01:31]: "if we press down the string between the nut, this white piece of plastic here, and the first fret, that piece of metal there, we get a note … represented by a one on the guitar tab."
- Visual: g_00080s.png (finger behind first fret), g_00090s.png (1–9 on top line).

**Partial:** States "open string" but omits nut/first fret, or vice versa.

## Q3 — Inlay frets, 12th fret, and how the 12th is marked
**Label: B**

**Answer:** Most guitars have inlays at the 3rd, 5th, 7th and 9th frets (all odd numbers). The 12th fret has a special inlay because every note at the 12th fret is exactly one octave above the open string. On the guitar shown, the 12th fret is marked with a double dot (two dots, one near each edge of the fretboard) instead of the single centered dot used at 3, 5, 7 and 9.

**Evidence:**
- Transcript [01:54]–[02:14]: "Most guitars have inlays … at the third, fifth, 7th, and ninth fret, which are all odd numbers. There's a special inlay at the 12th fret, and that's because all the notes on the 12th fret are exactly one octave higher than the open string."
- Visual: g_00110s.png, g_00120s.png, g_00130s.png — four single dots along the neck and two dots at the 12th fret (one high, one low). Also visible on the second guitar in g_00260s.png–g_00290s.png (black dots, double at the far left = 12th fret).

**Key terms (visual part):** double dot, two dots, twelfth/12th fret marker. The transcript never describes what the 12th-fret inlay looks like; "special inlay" only.

**Partial:** Frets and octave reason given but no description of the double dot (that is the spoken-only portion); or double dot described without the octave reason.
