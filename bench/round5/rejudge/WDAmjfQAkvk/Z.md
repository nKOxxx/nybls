# Answers

Source weighting: talk-heavy unboxing; the free transcript answered Q1, Q2, Q3, Q5 and Q7 fully and the verbal half of Q6 and Q8. Frames were spent only on the three visual gaps: case size on the box label (Q4), the watch face in the bezel comparison (Q6), and the sensor layouts (Q8).

## 1. Upgrade recommendation by current watch

- **Series 10, Series 11, Ultra 2 or Ultra 3: don't bother.** "It's not a big upgrade if at all" [08:43]–[08:49]; the only concrete gain he concedes is the new, differently laid-out sensors [08:51].
- **Series 9: probably upgrade, but only if your battery health is starting to be bad** [08:56]–[09:01].
- **Series 8 or older: definitely upgrade**, because the Series 8 (a 2022 watch) no longer supports the latest watchOS (transcribed as "watch of s27", i.e. watchOS 27) [09:04]–[09:11]; he calls dropping support on a four-year-old watch "crazy" and says he'd be angry having paid $400 for it [09:11]–[09:32].

Evidence: transcript [08:39]–[09:32].

## 2. Battery life: Series 12 vs 11 vs 10, and the 18 h → 24 h jump

- He has just unboxed it, so no measured figure; Apple rates the Series 12 at **24 hours, the same as the Series 11** [06:14]–[06:20]. His own guess: similar to the Series 11, maybe a bit better because the new chip is 2 nm and more efficient, "up to 36 hours or something, maybe two days" [06:42]–[06:51].
- **Series 10 was rated 18 hours.** He says there is not really a six-hour gain from 10 to 11; Apple simply changed how it calculates battery life so the rating now **includes six hours of sleep tracking** [06:24]–[06:37].

Evidence: transcript [06:09]–[06:51].

## 3. RAM

- **4 GB**, "a first in any Apple Watch" [05:48]–[05:52].
- Previous Apple Watches had **2 GB**, or **1 GB on the Series 8 or lower** [05:52]–[05:58].
- Why it matters: **future-proofing** — he holds up a four-year-old Series 8 that is already unsupported "simply because the chip is too slow and it only has one gig of RAM" [06:01]–[06:09].

Evidence: transcript [05:48]–[06:09].

## 5. Box contents and charging

In the box: the Apple Watch itself (in a bronze paper sleeve) [01:52], [03:21]; the **olive Sport Band, M/L** (he misspeaks "sport loop" then corrects to "sport band") [01:57]–[02:04], [02:17]; and the **charger cable** — USB-C, braided, one metre, "completely unchanged" [02:57]–[03:04]. ## 6. Bezels, and the watch face used

- **Thicker.** "The Series 12 has thicker bezels than the Series 10 and Series 11" [07:45]–[07:54]; a layperson would pick the older watch as the newer one because of its thinner bezels, which he calls "a bit disappointing" [07:54]–[08:06]. Earlier he had already noted the metal rim is thinner and more glass/bezel wraps around [03:50]–[04:09]. The side-by-side at [07:48] (`f_468000_1568.png`) is consistent with this: the Series 12 on the left shows a visibly wider black border around the face than the Series 10 (natural titanium, Milanese loop) on the right.
- **Watch face:** he says only "if we switch watch faces and go to this full screen one" [07:35]. On screen it is an edge-to-edge, full-screen face made of **rainbow-coloured radial wedges/pinwheel with plain white analog hands** (`f_460000_1568.png`, `f_468000_1568.png`). He does not name it, and I will not guess the Apple name of the face; the visual description is what the evidence supports.

Evidence: transcript [03:50]–[04:09], [07:31]–[08:08]; `f_460000_1568.png` [07:40], `f_468000_1568.png` [07:48].

## 8. Sensor array: Series 12 vs Series 11

Verbally he only says the new sensors are "laid out completely differently" [08:51]. Visually, at [08:53] (`f_533000_1568.png`, zooms `z_533000_36_40_20.png` and `z_533000_55_40_18.png`):
- **Series 12 (dark bronze, left):** the back is a flatter, matte surface with the usual "WATCH" engraving ring; the sensor window shows a single **compact circular cluster of ~8 small emitter/photodiode dots arranged in a tight ring** at the dead centre.
- **Series 11 (gold titanium, right):** polished dome with the familiar older layout — **a few larger, widely spaced sensor elements** (visible as separate bright points around the centre) rather than one tight central ring.

So the difference is a consolidated, small, centrally ringed array on the Series 12 versus the spread-out multi-element layout on the Series 11. The exact element count on the Series 11 is not resolvable from the frame (motion blur/reflections), so I report the layout pattern, not a count.

Evidence: transcript [03:34], [08:51]; `f_533000_1568.png` [08:53], `z_533000_36_40_20.png`, `z_533000_55_40_18.png`.
