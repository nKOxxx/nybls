## Q6 — Why Z.com failed; what changed for YouTube
**Label: S**

**Answer:** Z.com (an online entertainment company) failed because broadband penetration was too low in 1999–2000; it was too hard to watch video online (you had to install codecs in your browser). It went out of business in 2003. Two years later, (1) the codec problem had been solved by Adobe Flash and (2) broadband penetration had crossed 50 percent in America, so YouTube (2005) was perfectly timed — even though it had no business model at first.

**Evidence:**
- Transcript [05:19]–[05:57]: "We started a company called Z.com, it was an online entertainment company ... broadband penetration was too low in 1999-2000. It was too hard to watch video content online, you had to put codecs in your browser ... the company eventually went out of business in 2003. Just two years later, when the codec problem was solved by Adobe Flash and when broadband penetration crossed 50 percent in America, YouTube was perfectly timed ... YouTube didn't even have a business model when it first started."
- No dedicated slide; speaker on stage during this stretch (g_00325s.png–g_00355s.png).

**Partial:** broadband + 2003 but missing Flash or the 50% figure; or the two changes without the year.

## Q7 — How to assess timing
**Label: S** (summary slide supports it)

**Answer:** Look honestly at whether consumers are really ready for what you have to offer. Be really honest and not in denial about any results you see — because when you love something you want to push it forward, but you must be very honest about the timing factor.

**Evidence:**
- Transcript [06:06]–[06:20]: "the best way to really assess timing is to really look at whether consumers are really ready for what you have to offer them. And to be really, really honest about it, not be in denial about any results that you see, because if you have something you love, you want to push it forward, but you have to be very, very honest about that factor on timing."
- Summary slide "Execution matters a lot, but timing might matter even more" with the Timing icon enlarged: g_00365s.png, g_00385s.png.
