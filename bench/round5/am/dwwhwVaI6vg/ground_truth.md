## Q2 — Waking the yeast / sign it's working
**Label: S**

**Answer:** A little pinch of sugar or a little honey (optional) helps wake the yeast up. Mix with a fork, leave a couple of minutes, and you'll see bubbles starting — those bubbles are carbon dioxide, which becomes the bubbles in the spongy bread.

**Evidence (transcript):** [00:39]–[00:57] "Give it a nice little mix up with a fork. If you want to put a little pinch of sugar in there or a little honey, you can do that as well. It just helps to wake the yeast up. And if you leave it for a couple of minutes, you'll see the bubbles starting. Now, those bubbles are carbon dioxide". Frame g_00045s shows the fork-mixing.

**Partial:** sugar/honey named but no mention of bubbles (or vice versa).

## Q3 — Proving times and readiness
**Label: S**

**Answer:** First prove: put the dough ball in a bowl, cover with a damp cloth, about 1 hour to 1.5 hours, until doubled in size. After knocking back and shaping: second prove about half an hour to an hour, until at least twice as big, in a draught-free place. (He later says he proved both breads "until doubled in size".)

**Evidence (transcript):**
- [03:44]–[03:48] "cover it with like a damp cloth and in about an hour, hour and a half, this will happen"
- [03:36] "That's going to double in size"
- [05:31]–[05:35] "let that prove again for about half an hour to an hour and that will double in size"
- [05:40]–[05:44] "You want it to be at least twice as big at least. And just put it in a draft free place."
- [08:35]–[08:38] "I proved those two breads until doubled in size"
- Frames: g_00245s (ball in bowl), g_00255s (risen dough filling the bowl), g_00265s (tea towel over bowl).

**Partial:** only one of the two prove times; or times without the "doubled" criterion.
