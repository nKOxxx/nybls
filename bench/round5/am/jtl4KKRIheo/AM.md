# Answers — jtl4KKRIheo (iPhone 11 screen replacement)

Evidence used: transcript.txt and the two uniform stills in frames/ (am00_99s.png at 99.5 s, am01_298s.png at 298.4 s). Nothing else was opened.

## 1. Tools and screwdriver bits

**Answer (from transcript):** an iOpener, a set of iFixit opening picks, a suction handle or iSclack, a spudger, tweezers, and these bits: a P2 pentalobe bit, a Y000 (Y triple-zero) bit, and a Phillips bit.

**Evidence:** transcript [00:13]–[00:27] ("you'll need an eye opener ... ifixit opening picks ... a suction handle or ice clack a spudger tweezers and the following screwdriver bits a p tube pentalobe bit a y triple zero bit and a phillips"). The transcript's "eye opener", "ice clack" and "p tube" are speech-to-text renderings; the names iOpener, iSclack and P2 are inferred, with the P2 reading corroborated by [00:50] "two p2 pentalobe screws" and the iSclack reading corroborated visually by am00_99s.png, which shows a black tool handle with suction cups clamped on the phone edge whose moulded label reads "iSclack" (mirrored/upside-down in the frame).

Note: the video also says at [00:26]–[00:39] to have replacement waterproof seals/adhesive ready before proceeding; not a tool, but a stated prerequisite.

## 2. Battery before starting

**Answer (from transcript):** discharge the battery to below 25% before beginning. It matters because a charged lithium-ion battery can catch fire and/or explode if accidentally damaged during the repair.

**Evidence:** transcript [00:39]–[00:46] ("discharge your battery to below 25 / a charged lithium-ion battery can catch fire and or explode if you accidentally damage it"). The "%" is inferred; the transcript says only "below 25".

## 3. iOpener times

**Answer (from transcript):** bottom edge at the start: about one minute. Top front of the display (to soften the sensor adhesive): about one to two minutes.

**Evidence:** transcript [00:56]–[01:01] ("place a heated eye opener on the bottom edge of the phone and leave it there for about a minute"); [03:46]–[03:51] ("heat up the top front of the display for about one to two minutes so that we can soften the adhesive holding down the sensors").

## 4. Screw counts and bit

**Answer (from transcript):** battery connector bracket: three screws. Logic board cover bracket: five screws. Both use the Y000 (Y triple-zero) bit.

**Evidence:** transcript [02:42]–[02:45] ("you'll need your y triple zero bit to remove the three screws securing the battery connector in place"); [03:02]–[03:05] ("grab your y triple zero again and remove the five screws from the logic board cover bracket"). Neither frame shows these brackets, so counts are from the transcript only.

## 5. Cracked screen, suction won't grip

**Answer (from transcript):** (a) cover the display with a piece of clear packing tape so the suction cups have a surface to grip; (b) if you have an iSclack the step is easy; if you only have an iFixit suction handle, follow the opening procedure on the guide at ifixit.com (the video does not detail that alternative procedure). Also implied: heating with the iOpener first to soften the adhesive, so less lifting force is needed.

**Evidence:** transcript [01:02]–[01:17] ("if you have a cracked display suction cups might have a hard time attaching to the glass ... cover the display with a piece of clear packing tape if you have an ice clack on hand the next step will be easy but if you only have an ifixit suction handle follow the opening procedure on the guide at ifixit.com"). am00_99s.png shows the iSclack clamped on the bottom edge with a blue opening pick already inserted in the gap, matching [01:19]–[01:42]. No frame shows the packing-tape workaround.

## 6. Pick order and the clipped edge

**Answer (from transcript):** 1) Insert the pick in the gap at the bottom edge (made by the iSclack), slide it around the lower-left corner and up the left edge. 2) Re-insert at the bottom edge and slide up the right side. 3) The **top edge** is held by both adhesive and clips: gently pull the right side of the display down slightly toward the Lightning port, insert the pick at the top-right corner, and while still gently pulling down slide the pick across the top edge cutting the adhesive. Then open the display by swinging it up from the left side like a book's back cover, not lifting it fully (ribbon cables still attached).

**Evidence:** transcript [01:39]–[01:48] (bottom gap, lower-left corner, left edge); [01:59]–[02:06] (bottom again, up the right side); [02:06]–[02:20] (top edge adhesive and clips, pull right side toward Lightning port, pick in top-right corner, slide across top); [02:23]–[02:34] (swing up from the left). am00_99s.png shows the initial bottom-edge insertion. Reassembly note at [06:05]–[06:11] confirms clips along the top edge (align clips at top first).

## 7. Earpiece speaker / front sensor assembly

**Answer (from transcript):** Four screws hold it to the display: three Phillips and one Y000. Reinstall order on the new display: 1) press the proximity sensor and flood illuminator into their slots; 2) with tweezers slide the ambient light sensor back into place and put its bracket straight onto it; 3) press the microphone back into place with a finger; 4) press the flex cable below the microphone onto the display so it is well adhered (spudger OK); 5) flip the speaker assembly over and reinstall the four screws.

**Evidence:** transcript [03:27]–[03:34] ("remove the front assembly begin by removing four screws three being phillips and one y triple zero"); reassembly [04:36]–[05:06]. am01_298s.png (298 s, i.e. ~[04:58]) shows the top of a display with the earpiece speaker assembly flipped down/away and a fingertip pressing the flex cable area near the microphone/sensor region, and an empty screw hole visible at the top-right of the speaker, consistent with the "press the flex cable ... well adhered" step before screws go back in. I could not count screws or identify screw head types from the frame; types and count are from the transcript.
