## 1. Tools and screwdriver bits

**Answer:** iOpener, iFixit opening picks, a suction handle or iSclack, a spudger, tweezers; bits: P2 pentalobe, Y000 (tri-point), and Phillips.

**Evidence:** Transcript [00:13]–[00:27] lists "an eye opener, a set of ifixit opening picks, a suction handle or ice clack, a spudger, tweezers, and the following screwdriver bits: a p tube pentalobe bit, a y triple zero bit and a phillips". Frame `u01_19s.png` shows the on-screen caption "iOpener / iFixit Opening Picks / Suction Handle / iSclack / Spudger" with the iSclack, blue pick, driver, spudger, two tweezers and iOpener bag on the table.

**Saw vs inferred:** "p tube" is a transcription artifact; "P2" is inferred from [00:50] "two p2 pentalobe screws". The Phillips size is not stated in the transcript or readable in any frame (the frame caption lists tools, not bits), so I give no size for it.

## 2. Battery before starting

**Answer:** Discharge the battery to below 25% before beginning, then power the phone off. Reason given: a charged lithium-ion battery can catch fire and/or explode if you accidentally damage it during the repair.

**Evidence:** Transcript [00:39]–[00:48]: "discharge your battery to below 25 ... a charged lithium-ion battery can catch fire and or explode if you accidentally damage it ... power off your phone". Frame `u03_46s.png` shows the iPhone status bar reading "24%" (Control Center open), i.e. just under the threshold. Frame `u02_33s.png` is the red "WARNING!" card (the warning at [00:27] about waterproof seals precedes this).

## 3. iOpener timing

**Answer:** Bottom edge at the start: about one minute. Top front of the display when loosening the sensors later: about one to two minutes.

**Evidence:** Transcript [00:56]–[01:01] "place a heated eye opener on the bottom edge of the phone and leave it there for about a minute"; [03:46]–[03:51] "heat up the top front of the display for about one to two minutes so that we can soften the adhesive holding down the sensors". Frame `u04_59s.png` shows the iOpener laid across the bottom edge of the phone; frame `u17_232s.png` shows the iOpener laid across the top of the removed display.

## 4. Bracket screw counts and bit

**Answer:** Battery connector bracket: 3 screws. Logic board cover bracket: 5 screws. Both use the Y000 (tri-point) bit.

**Evidence:** Transcript [02:42]–[02:45] "you'll need your y triple zero bit to remove the three screws securing the battery connector"; [03:02]–[03:05] "grab your y triple zero again and remove the five screws from the logic board cover bracket". Frames `u12_165s.png`, `u13_179s.png`, `u14_192s.png` show the driver working on the brackets beside the battery; I did not count individual screw heads reliably from the stills, so the counts are from the transcript.

## 5. Cracked screen / suction cup won't grip

**Answer:** The narration gives one workaround: cover the display with a piece of clear packing tape so the suction cup can stick. The iFixit guide page shown on screen adds two more: use very sticky tape instead of the suction cup, or, if all else fails, superglue the suction cup to the broken screen. The video also notes that an iSclack makes the step easy, and if you only have a single suction handle you should follow the opening procedure in the guide at ifixit.com.

**Evidence:** Transcript [01:01]–[01:17]. Frame `u05_72s.png` shows the guide page text: "If your display is badly cracked, covering it with a layer of clear packing tape may help the suction cup to stick. Alternatively, you can use very sticky tape instead of the suction cup. Or if all else fails, you can superglue the suction cup to the broken screen."

**Saw vs inferred:** Packing tape is spoken; sticky-tape and superglue options are read from the on-screen guide page only, not narrated.

## 6. Pick order and the clipped edge

**Answer:** Order: (1) insert the pick in the gap at the bottom edge, slide around the lower-left corner and up the left edge; (2) re-insert at the bottom edge and slide up the right side; (3) top edge last. The top edge is held by clips as well as adhesive. Difference there: gently pull the right side of the display down slightly toward the Lightning port, insert the pick in the top-right corner, and while still gently pulling down slide the pick across the top edge to cut the adhesive. Then open the display by swinging it up from the left side like the back cover of a book, without lifting it fully off (ribbon cables still attached).

**Evidence:** Transcript [01:39]–[02:34]. Frames `u08_112s.png` (pick entering the left edge, bottom end), `u09_125s.png` (pick on the right edge), `u10_139s.png` (pick at the top-right corner while the display is held), `u11_152s.png` (display swung open to the right, connected by cables).

## 7. Earpiece speaker / front sensor assembly transfer

**Answer:** Four screws hold it to the display: three Phillips and one Y000. Reinstall order on the new display: (1) press the proximity sensor and flood illuminator into their slots; (2) with tweezers slide the ambient light sensor back into place and set its small bracket straight onto it; (3) press the microphone back into place with a finger; (4) press the flex cable below the microphone onto the display so it is well adhered (a spudger can be used); (5) flip the speaker assembly over and reinstall the four screws.

**Evidence:** Transcript [03:27]–[03:34] "removing four screws, three being phillips and one y triple zero"; reassembly [04:36]–[05:06]. Frames `u16_218s.png` (speaker assembly on the display top), `u18_245s.png`/`u19_258s.png` (speaker flipped down, sensors exposed, tweezers), `u20_271s.png` (pick under the flex cable), `u21_285s.png` (finger pressing a sensor into its notch), `u22_298s.png` (finger pressing flex cable), `u23_311s.png` (driver reinstalling a screw on the speaker assembly).

**Saw vs inferred:** Screw count and types are from narration; I cannot distinguish Phillips from tri-point heads in the stills.
