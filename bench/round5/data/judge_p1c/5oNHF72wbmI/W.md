1. What channel branding appears on screen, and on what object is it visible?

YouTube branding appears on a custom license plate. The creator's YouTube license plate is visibly shown on the McLaren, although it is registered to the "Mr. Beast Lamborghini." Additionally, merchandise branding for "edgasket.com" appears on custom t-shirts designed by the creator, featuring a Philadelphia "love" logo where the "O" resembles a McLaren wheel, along with American and British flags.

2. Is a phone or messaging screenshot shown at any point? What is it about?

Yes, a text message exchange is shown on screen. The creator texts the CEO of Diagcode (Miro) asking which unit to enter to program a new key fob. The CEO responds with "Hey, here. Key programming, found it." This exchange occurs when the creator is attempting to program his second key fob to the McLaren using the Diagcode diagnostic software.

3. What is shown on screen relating to the car's keys?

The disassembled key fob is a central visual element, shown in three separate pieces. The original key fob that arrived at the auction house came in multiple pieces, which served as a clue about what happened at the auction. Later, a new/second key fob is shown in its packaging after being purchased from another McLaren owner. The creator demonstrates holding buttons on the new key fob during the key programming process with Diagcode software, and shows both keys working by starting the car.

4. What tool is used to read the car's electronics, and how does it appear?

Initially, the $5,000 McLaren dealer diagnostic tool is used—a tablet-like device with specialized software. Later, a second tool called Diagcode (made by Miro, a company from Sofia, Bulgaria) is introduced. Diagcode is a simpler device that plugs into the car's OBD-II port via USB and runs diagnostic software on a tablet interface. Diagcode appears as a small, more user-friendly alternative that successfully connects to the McLaren where the dealer tool fails. The Diagcode software is shown on screen with graphical interfaces for reading fault codes and programming features.

5. What vehicles other than the McLaren appear in the video?

An orange McLaren (belonging to Kevin from Marchetti Automotive/Supercar Garage) appears when Kevin drives it to the creator's front door to deliver the diagnostic tool. A silver/gray Subaru appears in the background when the creator illegally drives his unregistered McLaren to the inspection station—driven by an elderly woman who unknowingly serves as cover from potential police attention.
